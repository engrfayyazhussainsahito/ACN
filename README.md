## Overview

**Advanced Computer Networks (ACN)** is an open-source educational platform designed to make advanced networking concepts understandable, implementable, and experimentally verifiable.

Rather than presenting networking as a collection of isolated protocols and theoretical definitions, ACN approaches the subject as an interconnected engineering discipline.

The platform brings together:

- **Theory:** In-depth technical explanations, mathematical foundations, protocol mechanisms, and architectural principles.
- **Visualization:** Interactive diagrams, computed figures, and visual representations of complex networking concepts.
- **Implementation:** Hands-on laboratories, practical experiments, and programming-based exercises.
- **Evaluation:** Problem-solving exercises, worked solutions, and assessment-oriented practice.
- **Research:** Contemporary networking technologies, research directions, and emerging architectural paradigms.

The objective is simple: bridge the gap between understanding how networks work and developing the ability to analyze, design, implement, and evaluate them.

## Curriculum Architecture

The curriculum is organized into five progressive learning phases.

### Phase I — Network Foundations & Architecture

Establishing the theoretical and mathematical foundations required to understand modern network systems.

- Internet Architecture and Design Principles
- Queueing Theory and Performance Modelling
- Routing Algorithms: Link-State and Distance-Vector
- Border Gateway Protocol (BGP)

### Phase II — Transport Protocols & Congestion Control

Understanding transport-layer mechanisms, performance optimization, and modern transport architectures.

- Congestion Control: AIMD, TCP Reno and CUBIC
- BBR and Delay-Based Congestion Control
- QUIC and TLS 1.3
- Multipath Transport

### Phase III — Data Center Networks & Programmability

Exploring the infrastructure and programmable architectures powering modern network environments.

- Data Center Network Architectures
- Software-Defined Networking (SDN)
- Programmable Data Planes
- Network Control and Forwarding

### Phase IV — Wireless, Content Delivery & Network Security

Examining network access technologies, content distribution, and security challenges.

- Wireless and Mobile Networking
- Content Delivery Networks (CDNs)
- Video Streaming Architectures
- Network Security: DDoS, DNS, TLS and BGP
- Network Measurement
- Satellite Networks and Future Directions

### Phase V — Emerging Networking Paradigms

Investigating the networking technologies supporting next-generation computing infrastructure.

- AI Cluster Networking
- RDMA and Collective Communication
- WAN Traffic Engineering
- MPLS and Network Overlays
- Naming Architectures
- Final Projects and Research-Oriented Applications

## What Makes ACN Different?

### 1. Theory-Driven Learning

Every major topic is supported by technical explanations, underlying mechanisms, mathematical formulations, and worked examples.

The emphasis is on understanding *why* a protocol or architecture works, not merely memorizing its operation.

### 2. Visual Learning

Complex networking mechanisms are supported by diagrams, computed figures, and interactive demonstrations to make abstract concepts easier to investigate.

### 3. Hands-On Laboratories

The platform includes practical experiments designed to translate theoretical knowledge into implementation experience.

Students are encouraged to configure, execute, observe, measure, and analyze networking behavior.

### 4. Problem-Solving & Assessment

Exercises and worked solutions reinforce conceptual understanding, analytical reasoning, and technical problem-solving.

### 5. Research-Oriented Content

Beyond conventional networking topics, ACN explores modern research areas, including programmable networks, AI infrastructure networking, satellite communication, and advanced transport architectures.

### 6. AI-Powered Course Assistant

An integrated course chatbot provides an additional way to explore course concepts and seek assistance while studying.

## Intended Audience

ACN is designed for:

- Undergraduate students seeking advanced networking knowledge.
- MS Computer Science and related graduate students.
- Computer networking and infrastructure engineers.
- Researchers exploring modern networking architectures.
- Educators looking for structured teaching and laboratory resources.

A basic understanding of the following subjects is recommended:

- Computer Networks and TCP/IP.
- Operating Systems.
- Basic Probability and Statistics.
- Mathematical Foundations.

## Project Philosophy

Networking should not be learned exclusively through definitions, diagrams, or examinations.

It should be learned through a combination of mathematical reasoning, architectural understanding, practical implementation, experimentation, and critical analysis.

ACN is built around this philosophy.

The long-term vision is to develop an accessible, continuously improving knowledge base that helps learners move from foundational networking concepts to advanced engineering and research-level understanding.
