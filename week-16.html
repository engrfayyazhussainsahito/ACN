Final Projects, Review and Exam

Project talks, reproducibility and a final review of the whole semester.

Based on: All sources

Learning objectives
  • Explain control/data plane separation and its trade-offs.
  • Compute Shannon capacity for a given bandwidth and SNR.
  • Model cache hit ratio for Zipf-distributed demand.
  • Compute reflected attack bandwidth from an amplification factor.
  • Compute propagation-limited RTTs for fibre, LEO and GEO.
  • Derive the cost of ring and recursive halving-doubling all-reduce.
  • Formulate WAN traffic engineering as a multicommodity-flow problem.

Final exam

Cumulative, with emphasis on Weeks 9 to 15: sketch sizing, all-reduce cost, max-min fairness, Shannon capacity, Zipf caching, amplification, propagation delay.

Reproducibility checklist

Before submitting: state the topology, link rates, delays, buffer sizes and workload; fix and report random seeds; run at least 5 seeds and plot error bars; include the baselines the community expects (Reno or CUBIC, BBR, DCTCP, ECMP as appropriate); release code and configuration; and report negative results and failure cases.

How to read a networking paper in 30 minutes

Read the abstract and conclusion, then the figures; decide the claim; read the evaluation setup and ask what is missing; only then read the design. Write down the baseline, the metric, one threat to validity and one follow-up experiment. This is also the template for your weekly paper critiques.

Project menu

Choose one scope: (1) reproduce a figure from a SIGCOMM/NSDI paper in Mininet or ns-3; (2) compare Reno, CUBIC and BBR under varying buffer size and RTT; (3) measure QUIC versus TCP page-load time on emulated lossy links; (4) implement a count-min sketch heavy-hitter detector in P4 or Python on a packet trace; (5) analyse BGP table dynamics from RouteViews/RIPE RIS data. Each project needs a hypothesis, an experimental plan and a failure analysis.

Practice final exam (selected questions)

Solve these before looking at the solutions in the exercise list: pipeline latency, M/M/1, the Mathis formula, Chord hop count and all-reduce bandwidth term.

Final review checklist

For each of Weeks 9 to 15 you should be able to: state the problem, give the key formula or algorithm, work a numeric example by hand, explain one design trade-off, and name two papers. Then revisit the midterm topics: the final is cumulative.

Exercises

Exercise 1: Practice: pick one paper from Weeks 4 to 7 and state its claim, method, a baseline you would add and one threat to validity.

Open-ended: a good answer names a specific confound (e.g. a single topology, one seed, a buffer size favouring one scheme).

Exercise 2: Why is reporting only the mean of 3 runs insufficient?

Three samples give almost no estimate of variance; report ≥5 seeds with confidence intervals or percentile bands.

Exercise 3: Derive T for a message of P packets through N store-and-forward links.

The first packet needs N·L/R; each of the other P−1 packets follows one transmission time later: T = (N + P − 1)·L/R.

Exercise 4: M/M/1 with μ = 500 pkt/s, ρ = 0.8: W and L?

λ = 400; W = 1/(500−400) = 10 ms; L = 4.

Exercise 5: Mathis: RTT 50 ms, MSS 1460 B, p = 10⁻³. Throughput?

1.22 × 11,680 / 0.05 / 0.0316 ≈ 9.0 Mb/s.

Exercise 6: Chord with 2²⁰ nodes: average lookup hops?

½·20 = 10.

Exercise 7: Ring all-reduce, N = 16, S = 4 GB, B = 25 GB/s: bandwidth term?

2·(15/16)·4/25 = 0.30 s.

Exercise 8: Heavy hitters: a count-min sketch with w = 1024 and d = 4 processes N = 10⁶ packets. By how much can a flow's estimate exceed its true count (with probability ≥ 1 − e⁻⁴)?

ε = e/w ≈ 0.00266, so at most εN ≈ 2,656 packets.

Exercise 9: A 20 MHz Wi-Fi link at 30 dB SNR: Shannon capacity?

20·log₂(1001) ≈ 199 Mb/s.

Exercise 10: A CDN cache hits 95% of the time (hit 5 ms, miss 100 ms). Mean latency and origin offload?

0.95·5 + 0.05·100 = 9.75 ms; origin sees 5% of requests (20× offload).

Exercise 11: Water-filling: capacity 30, demands 5, 10, 20, 20.

(5, 8.33, 8.33, 8.33).

Exercise 12: Chord with 2²⁰ nodes: mean hops, and why not N/2?

About 10 hops; fingers at distances 2ⁱ halve the remaining distance each hop, whereas successor pointers move one node at a time.
