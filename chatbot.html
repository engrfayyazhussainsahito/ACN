Ask the course

A chatbot grounded in every page of this site. It retrieves the relevant passages, optionally has an AI model write an answer from them, cites the sections it used, and says so when the course does not cover a question.

How the RAG pipeline works

  1. Index (build time). Every section, worked example, exercise with solution, lab, glossary term, appendix, assignment and reading list is split into passages (about 690 in total).
  2. Retrieve (in your browser). Your question is expanded with acronyms and synonyms and the passages are ranked with BM25 plus phrase matching. If nothing in the course is a confident match, the chatbot stops there and says so; the AI model is never asked to guess.
  3. Augment. The best passages (and the continuation of the top one) are numbered and packed into a prompt together with the last few turns of the conversation.
  4. Generate. A language model writes the answer using only those passages and cites them as [1], [2]. The answer streams in word by word.
  5. Check. Citations are turned into links to the exact section, invalid citations are dropped, and any number in the answer that does not appear in the retrieved text is flagged for you to check.

Choose how answers are generated (the ⚙ AI mode button)

  • Course search only (default unless the site owner configured AI): no model; you get the best passage and links. Works offline.
  • In-browser AI: a small open model (about 1–3 billion parameters) runs on your own GPU through WebGPU. Free and private, but the first question downloads the model (hundreds of MB to a few GB, then cached) and quality is that of a small model.
  • Cloud AI via the course proxy: best quality. The site owner deploys the small proxy in worker/ (instructions in worker/README.md), which holds the API key and calls a Claude model.
  • OpenAI-compatible endpoint: for example Ollama or LM Studio running on your own computer, or any provider with a chat-completions API.

What to ask

  • Concepts: “What is head-of-line blocking?”, “Why does CUBIC beat Reno on long paths?”.
  • Calculations: “Walk me through the Mathis formula”, “Little’s law example”.
  • Navigation: “What is in Week 9?”, “show the syllabus”, “which lab simulates ECMP collisions?”.
  • Practice: “Quiz me on BGP” (then use the Show solution button).
  • Study help: “Which papers should I read for congestion control?”, “assignment for week 4”.

Limits

Answers are only as good as the retrieved passages: a very broad question may need several sections, and a small in-browser model can still mis-explain a passage it was given, so follow the citations for anything important. In search-only mode the chatbot quotes rather than reasons.

Training vs RAG

RAG does not retrain a model: the knowledge lives in the index and is rebuilt with the site, so answers stay current with the course and are traceable to a section. If you want to fine-tune a model anyway, training/acn-qa-pairs.jsonl contains the exercises, glossary and week summaries in chat format.
