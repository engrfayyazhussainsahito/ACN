Open course · MS level · 16 weeks

Understand networks the way they are actually built.

A graduate course in advanced computer networking: from queueing and routing to congestion control, QUIC, data centers, SDN, wireless, CDNs, security and satellites. Each chapter runs from the basics to advanced material, with derivations, worked examples, computed charts, interactive demos, runnable labs, exercises with solutions, a programming assignment and a paper-reading guide. Appendices cover the maths, networking fundamentals and lab tools, and every chapter is available as a PDF.

01Internet Architecture and Design Principles

Layering, the narrow waist, the end-to-end argument and the delay model that every later week builds on.

02Queueing Theory and Performance Modelling

Little's law, M/M/1 and M/D/1 queues, why delay explodes near full utilisation and why real traffic breaks Poisson assumptions.

03Routing: Link-State, Distance-Vector and BGP

Shortest-path algorithms, the count-to-infinity problem, and BGP's policy-driven interdomain routing, including the 2008 YouTube hijack.

04Congestion Control I: AIMD, Reno and CUBIC

Why AIMD converges to fairness, how Reno and CUBIC differ, and why loss-based control struggles on large-BDP paths.

05Congestion Control II: BBR, Delay-Based and Modelling

Throughput models, bufferbloat, and model-based control (BBR) that targets the bandwidth-delay product instead of loss.

06Modern Transport: QUIC, TLS 1.3 and Multipath

Why transport moved to user space over UDP, how handshakes shrink from four round trips to one or zero, and head-of-line blocking.

07Data Center Networks

Clos and fat-tree topologies, ECMP and its collisions, incast, and DCTCP.

08Midterm Review and Exam

A cumulative review of Weeks 1 to 7 with a formula sheet and practice questions.

09SDN and Programmable Data Planes

Separating control and data planes, match-action abstractions, P4, and in-network telemetry with sketches.

10Wireless and Mobile Networking

Shannon capacity, fading and rate adaptation, 802.11 contention and why wireless TCP is hard.

11Content Delivery and Video Streaming

CDN architecture, Zipf popularity and caching, DNS-based redirection and adaptive bitrate control.

12Network Security: DDoS, DNS, TLS and BGP

Reflection and amplification attacks, SYN floods, DNSSEC, TLS 1.3 and securing interdomain routing with RPKI.

13Measurement, Satellite Networks and the Future

How the Internet is measured, LEO constellations versus fibre latency, and open research directions.

14AI Cluster Networking: RDMA, Collectives and Rail Design

Why distributed training reshaped data-center networks: synchronous collectives, ring versus tree all-reduce, RoCE and its congestion control, and rail-optimised topologies.

15WAN Traffic Engineering, MPLS, Overlays and Naming

Label switching, traffic engineering as a flow optimisation problem, max-min fairness, structured overlays (Chord) and the DNS hierarchy.

16Final Projects, Review and Exam

Project talks, reproducibility and a final review of the whole semester.

How to study

  1. Read each derivation and try the worked example on paper first.
  2. Run every lab and change one parameter at a time.
  3. Do the exercises before opening the solutions.
  4. Read the Essential papers: networking is a paper-driven field.

Prerequisites: an undergraduate networking course (TCP/IP), probability, Python.
