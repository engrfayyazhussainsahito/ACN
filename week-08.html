Midterm Review and Exam

A cumulative review of Weeks 1 to 7 with a formula sheet and practice questions.

Based on: All sources

Learning objectives
  • Explain why the Internet's narrow waist enabled growth and also froze IP.
  • Apply Little's law to any stable system.
  • Run Dijkstra and Bellman-Ford and explain their message costs.
  • Prove with Chiu-Jain geometry that AIMD converges to fair, efficient shares.
  • Use the Mathis formula to predict TCP throughput.
  • Count round trips for TCP+TLS and QUIC setups.
  • Size a k-ary fat-tree.

Format

A 75-minute in-class exam: five short derivations, one worked calculation per topic, and one paper-critique question. Weeks 1 to 7 only.

Extra practice problems

Work these under exam conditions, then check the solutions in the exercise list below. Together with the formula sheet they cover the main calculation types from Weeks 1 to 7: store-and-forward delay, AIMD recovery time, fat-tree sizing, Mathis throughput, M/M/1 queues and handshake round trips.

How to prepare

Re-derive from memory: the packetised transfer time, M/M/1 mean delay, the Reno throughput formula, the fat-tree counts and the BGP decision order. Redo every exercise without looking at the solution. Re-run each lab and predict its output before reading it.

Exercises

Exercise 1: Practice: a 100 ms RTT path with p = 10⁻⁶ and MSS 1460 B. Predict Reno throughput and compare with 10 Gb/s.

1.22×11680/0.1/0.001 ≈ 142 Mb/s: about 1.4% of the link; loss-based Reno cannot fill it.

Exercise 2: Practice: Why can M/M/1 delay at 95% load be 10x that at 50% load?

W = 1/(μ−λ): (1−0.5)/(1−0.95) = 10.

Exercise 3: A 1000 B packet crosses 5 hops at 10 Mb/s (store-and-forward, ignore propagation). End-to-end transmission delay?

5 × (8000/10⁷) = 4 ms.

Exercise 4: A Reno flow halves cwnd from 120 to 60 after a loss. How many RTTs to recover?

Additive increase of 1 per RTT: 60 RTTs.

Exercise 5: A fat-tree uses 8-port switches. Hosts and switches?

k³/4 = 128 hosts; 5k²/4 = 80 switches.

Exercise 6: Which of TCP+TLS 1.3 and QUIC 1-RTT saves how much at RTT 80 ms?

3 RTT vs 2 RTT: saves 80 ms.

Exercise 7: A 4 MB file crosses 4 store-and-forward links at 100 Mb/s using 1000 B packets (ignore propagation). Transfer time?

P = 4000 packets; T = (4 + 4000 − 1)·8000/10⁸ = 0.320 s.

Exercise 8: M/M/1, λ = 800, μ = 1000 pkt/s. Mean delay, mean number in system, and P(more than 10 packets in the system)?

W = 1/200 = 5 ms; L = 4; P(>10) = ρ¹¹ = 0.8¹¹ = 8.6%.

Exercise 9: x–y costs 3, y–z costs 4, x–z costs 9. What does distance-vector give for x's distance to z?

min(9, 3 + 4) = 7, via y.

Exercise 10: A Reno flow halves its window from 200 to 100 at an RTT of 200 ms. Time to regain 200?

100 RTTs × 0.2 s = 20 s.

Exercise 11: RTT 120 ms. Time to first byte for TCP+TLS 1.3, QUIC 1-RTT and QUIC 0-RTT?

3 × 120 = 360 ms; 2 × 120 = 240 ms; 1 × 120 = 120 ms.
