Assignments

One programming assignment per content week. Each is designed to take 4 to 8 hours and to reproduce a figure or result from the notes.

Week 1: Internet Architecture and Design Principles

Build a small discrete-event simulator for store-and-forward links in Python and reproduce Figure 1 (transfer time versus hops). Then add propagation delay and per-hop processing and show for which link rates (1 Mb/s, 1 Gb/s, 100 Gb/s) transmission, propagation or processing dominates. Deliver code, a figure and a one-page explanation.

Week 2: Queueing Theory and Performance Modelling

Write a discrete-event simulator of an M/M/1 and an M/M/1/K queue. Compare mean delay and loss with the formulas at ρ = 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, with confidence intervals from at least 10 runs. Then replace Poisson arrivals by ON/OFF sources with Pareto ON periods (shape 1.5) at the same mean load and show how the delay changes.

Week 3: Routing: Link-State, Distance-Vector and BGP

Implement (a) a link-state simulator (flooding + Dijkstra) and (b) a distance-vector simulator with and without split horizon and poisoned reverse. On a 10-node topology fail a link and plot messages sent and convergence time. Then, using a RouteViews or RIPE RIS RIB snapshot (or the toy file provided), compute the distribution of AS-path lengths.

Week 4: Congestion Control I: AIMD, Reno and CUBIC

Write a per-RTT simulator for N flows sharing a bottleneck with Reno and CUBIC. Reproduce Chiu-Jain convergence using Jain's fairness index, measure RTT unfairness (20 ms vs 80 ms flows), and plot average utilisation versus buffer size of 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 × BDP.

Week 5: Congestion Control II: BBR, Delay-Based and Modelling

In Mininet use tc/netem and iperf3 to run CUBIC and BBR across buffer sizes (0.1 to 8 × BDP) and RTTs (20 and 100 ms). Record goodput, retransmissions and RTT inflation, plot, and explain each regime. Repeat with fq_codel at the bottleneck.

Week 6: Modern Transport: QUIC, TLS 1.3 and Multipath

Write a toy reliable transport over UDP with sequence numbers, ACK ranges, an RTT-based retransmission timer and independent streams. Fetch 20 objects over one ordered stream and over independent streams under 0 to 3% loss (netem) and plot page-load time.

Week 7: Data Center Networks

Build a k = 4 fat-tree (Mininet or a flow-level simulator). On a permutation traffic matrix compare ECMP, per-packet spraying and flowlet switching and plot maximum link utilisation and the completion-time CDF. Add an incast generator (8 to 64 senders) and sweep the ECN threshold K with DCTCP-style reaction.

Week 9: SDN and Programmable Data Planes

Write a behavioural model (P4_16 or Python) that does longest-prefix forwarding and a count-min heavy-hitter detector. Run it on a Zipf trace and report precision and recall for the top 50 flows versus sketch width. Then build a two-phase consistent update in a simulator and show a transient loop under a naive update.

Week 10: Wireless and Mobile Networking

Implement the link-budget calculator and then an 802.11 CSMA/CA simulator (CWmin 15, CWmax 1023, binary exponential backoff). Plot saturation throughput and collision probability versus number of stations and compare with Bianchi's model.

Week 11: Content Delivery and Video Streaming

Build a trace-driven cache simulator (LRU, LFU and a TinyLFU-style admission filter) on a synthetic Zipf trace and plot object and byte hit ratios versus cache size. Then implement buffer-based and throughput-based ABR over recorded throughput traces and compare QoE.

Week 12: Network Security: DDoS, DNS, TLS and BGP

In an isolated Mininet lab (never against real hosts), build a server with a finite backlog, show how a SYN flood exhausts it and implement SYN cookies. Measure the amplification factor of a toy UDP service you write yourself. Then classify prefixes in a published RPKI validator dump as valid, invalid or unknown.

Week 13: Measurement, Satellite Networks and the Future

Write a traceroute-style probe with scapy (or use RIPE Atlas) to 10 destinations. Compute RTT percentiles and compare with the fibre propagation lower bound; plot time-of-day variation. Then reproduce the LEO-versus-fibre crossover for your own stretch assumptions.

Week 14: AI Cluster Networking: RDMA, Collectives and Rail Design

Using the all-reduce model and the demo, size a fabric: for a 1,024-GPU job with 10 GB gradients, compare ring and halving-doubling at 200, 400 and 800 Gb/s per GPU. Then write a flow-level simulator of ECMP with 8 elephant flows and measure the distribution of the maximum link load over 10,000 random hash assignments.

Week 15: WAN Traffic Engineering, MPLS, Overlays and Naming

Implement water-filling max-min fairness and a path-splitting TE solver (use scipy.optimize.linprog if available, otherwise a greedy heuristic) on the Abilene topology or a toy 6-node graph. Then implement Chord finger tables in simulation and measure lookup hops for N = 2⁸ to 2¹⁴ against ½·log₂N.
